A redesigned homepage is not news. What a competitor *hires for*, *charges*, and
*stops doing* is.

Rank what you find by how hard it was to fake:

1. **Price changes and packaging changes.** They cost the competitor something to
   make and they move the market you sell into.
2. **Job postings.** A team hiring for a new surface is telling you what ships in
   two quarters, before any announcement does.
3. **Review sites.** Read the two-star reviews, not the one-star ones: one-star
   is usually billing, two-star is the product limit you can name in outreach.
4. **Announcements and launches.** Cheap to publish, so weigh them last.

Write what it means for *this* company or do not write it. "Competitor X raised
prices 20%" is a fact; "X raised prices 20%, so our €9 tier is now the only one
under €15 in this segment" is a finding the CEO can act on.

Never copy a claim you have not seen the evidence for, and never carry a
competitor's number into our own copy. If nothing moved this cycle, say nothing
moved. A watch that always reports something stops being read.
