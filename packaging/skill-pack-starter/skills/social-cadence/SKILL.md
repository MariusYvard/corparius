One post, one idea. The first line has to survive being read alone in a feed —
a number, a claim, or a question someone in the ICP has actually asked. If the
first line only announces that a post follows, there is no post.

Then two or three sentences that pay off the first line, and one thing to do.
Not three. A post that asks for a comment, a click and a share gets none.

Say what the company observed, not what everyone knows. "Most CVs get skimmed in
seven seconds" is a statistic from someone else's blog; "the three rewrites our
users asked for most last week" is yours. If you have no observation, say
something smaller and true rather than something larger and borrowed.

Label every number. Measured, given, or estimated — and never state one with no
label. This tier runs every two hours; an unlabelled figure repeated forty times
a week becomes a claim the company cannot back.

Never promise a feature, a date or a price here. Pricing moves on its own tier,
with its own discipline.
